# Dates and periods

The most common way to get a benefits answer wrong is to give last year's number. Do these checks for every answer.

## 1. Know today's date
Start from today's date. "Current" means the rule in effect today, not the rule you remember.

## 2. Know which kind of period the question uses
Different programs update on different schedules:
- **Federal fiscal year** (October 1 to September 30): many federal food program amounts update on October 1.
- **July 1 to June 30**: some nutrition program income guidelines run on this year.
- **School year**: school meal guidelines follow the school year.
- **Heating season**: energy assistance runs by winter season, with its own opening and closing dates.
- **Calendar year**: some federal payment amounts update every January.
- **Tax year**: tax credits apply to the tax year being filed, which is usually the year before the filing date.
- **Federal poverty guidelines**: published once a year. Percent-of-poverty limits change when the new guidelines are adopted.

Don't assume which schedule a program uses. Confirm it on the official page.

## 3. Match the source to the period
- Find the date on the source: a title like "Effective October 1, 2026," a school year label, a tax year, or a "last updated" date.
- If the question names a period, the source must cover that exact period.
- If the question says "current," the source must cover today's date.
- If you can only find an older version, don't use it as the current answer. Keep searching, or abstain.

## 4. Know the period of every answer
Before you write an answer, be able to say which period it applies to (for example "FY2027 (Oct 1 2026 - Sep 30 2027)" or "tax year 2025"). If the source doesn't say, lower your confidence.

## 5. Future questions
If the question asks about a future value or decision that hasn't been officially announced, abstain. Predictions are not answers.
